Welcome to the glaze room here at Kestrel Clay Studio. Before you dip a single pot, remember that everything on these shelves is formulated for a cone 6 stoneware firing, which reaches about 1222 degrees Celsius in our big electric kiln.

Every piece you glaze today has already been bisque fired to cone 04, so it is hard but still porous, and that porosity is what lets the raw glaze grab and dry onto the wall of the pot in seconds.

We have three buckets open on the bench. The first is a classic shino, which breaks a warm toasty orange where it runs thin over an edge. The second is a deep glossy tenmoku that pools almost black and thins to a rich rust brown. The third is a soft milky nuka that we love over dark clay bodies.

Application is mostly about thickness and timing. For most of these glazes you want a coat about as thick as a credit card. On our buckets that means a slow three second dip, a steady count, and then lift the pot straight out and let it drip back into the bucket.

Before you dip, wax the foot ring and the bottom of every pot with our blue resist. Wax makes the glaze bead up and run right off the foot, which keeps molten glaze from gluing your pot to the kiln shelf and ruining both.

Speaking of shelves, we brush a thin layer of kiln wash on every shelf for exactly the same reason. If a drip does land on a washed shelf, it chips off cleanly instead of welding itself to the surface forever.

The defect you will fight most as a beginner is called crawling, where the glaze pulls back into little islands and leaves bare clay showing through. Crawling almost always comes from dust, oil, or a fingerprint left on the bisque, so handle clean pots with clean dry hands.

If you are curious what is actually in the bucket, most of these recipes are built from the same three families of ingredient: silica for the glass, a feldspar to help it melt, and whiting to stiffen the melt and adjust the surface.

Wipe the foot one last time with a damp sponge, load your pots so nothing touches its neighbor, and we will unload this glaze firing together on Thursday afternoon.
